## AI-Powered CURA Application for Identifying At-Risk Pregnancies in Obstetrics Management (CURAte): protocol for a randomised controlled trial.

- 来源: PubMed · 推送 09:10
- 期刊: BMJ Open · 中科院医学4区(医学：内科4区) · IF 2.5 · JCR Q2
- 国家/单位: National University Hospital · National University Hospital
- 通讯作者: Li S (ling.sarah@gmail.com)
- DOI: 10.1136/bmjopen-2026-124115
- 获取全文: [DOI 全文](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2026-124115) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42901589/)

**中文总结**：- 研究目的：评估AI辅助产前风险分层相较标准产前管理能否提高高危/低危妊娠分类准确性，并降低孕产妇及胎儿/新生儿不良结局复合发生率。
- 核心发现/数据：本研究为随机对照试验方案，尚未报告结局数据；计划纳入1700名孕妇，按1:1区组随机分组，并按族裔和产次分层，参与者对分组设盲。AI风险评估在早孕期产检及31–33周两个时点进行，主要复合结局于妊娠结束后6周从病历提取并按意向性治疗分析。背景数据显示常规早孕期高危妊娠识别率仅约20%–30%，目前尚无机器学习产前风险分类方法在RCT中与标准照护比较。
- 临床启示：若试验证实有效，AI辅助风险分层可作为临床决策支持工具，优化高危孕妇转诊与分层管理，但不替代临床判断；还需评估不同人口学和产科亚组的差异。

**原文摘要**：Early identification of high-risk pregnancies is a cornerstone of obstetric care, yet antenatal risk assessment in routine practice remains imprecise and highly variable. Evidence from contemporary cohort studies suggests that only a minority of pregnancies that subsequently experience adverse maternal or fetal/neonatal outcomes are identified as high risk in the first trimester, with reported detection rates in the range of 20%–30%. Despite rapid advances in machine-learning (ML) methods for clinical risk prediction, no ML-based approach for general early antenatal risk categorisation has yet been evaluated against standard care within a randomised controlled trial (RCT) to assess impact on maternity outcomes. We hypothesise that adoption of an artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted antenatal risk stratification approach will improve the accuracy of categorising pregnancies into high-risk and low-risk groups and will reduce the composite incidence of adverse maternal and fetal/neonatal outcomes. We will conduct an RCT comparing outcomes between participants allocated to an intervention arm employing AI-assisted risk stratification and those in the control arm receiving standard antenatal care without AI support. 1700 participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio using block randomisation, stratified by ethnicity and parity. Participants will be blinded to allocation. AI-assisted risk assessment using maternal sociodemographic, clinical and obstetrical data, will be applied at two time points: (1) a first trimester antenatal visit and (2) between 31 and 33 weeks’ gestation. AI categorisation outputs (intervention: ‘high-risk’ or ‘low-risk’; control: ‘undisclosed’) will be displayed to attending clinicians at both time points, and subsequent management will follow a predefined clinical protocol, one written for pregnancies categorised as high-risk and one for low-risk. For any pregnancy categorised as high-risk, the clinician works through predefined referral criteria for the maternal-fetal medicine-led high-risk clinic and completes the specified actionable investigations and interventions before referral. Participants are followed until 6 weeks after the end of pregnancy. The primary composite outcome is extracted from medical records 6 weeks after the end of pregnancy. The two arms are compared on an intention-to-treat basis once the database has been locked. There is no interim analysis of the primary outcome. Ethical approval was obtained from the National Healthcare Group Domain Specific Review Board (ECOS 2024-3832). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants, with data handled in accordance with institutional data-protection requirements and the Personal Data Protection Act. The AI tool functions as clinical decision support and does not replace clinician judgement; subgroup analyses will assess potential differential effects across key demographic and obstetric groups.
